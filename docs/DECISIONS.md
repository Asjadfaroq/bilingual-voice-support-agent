# Decisions

- STT is ElevenLabs Scribe v2 realtime (`scribe_v2_realtime`) instead of Deepgram, which is not available; `livekit-plugins-elevenlabs` 1.8.10 streams it over a websocket and supports `secondary_languages` and per-transcript language detection, which suits Arabic/English switching.
- TTS is ElevenLabs `eleven_flash_v2_5` (lowest-latency multilingual model) with the premade voice "Sarah" (`EXAVITQu4vr4xnSDxMaL`), which ElevenLabs verifies for Arabic and English.
- HubSpot uses a service key on the EU host `api-eu1.hubapi.com`; creating a call engagement works with only the contacts and tickets scopes (tested 2026-10-29: `POST /crm/v3/objects/calls` returned 201, then deleted), so calls are logged as real call objects, not notes.
- `torch` comes from the CPU wheel index: the embedding model runs on CPU and the CUDA build grew the venv from 1.5 GB to 5.8 GB.
- `kb_min_similarity` (0.72) is a noise floor, not the "found / not found" decision: measured e5 scores overlap (Arabic in-domain questions from 0.756, English off-topic up to 0.803), so the LLM judges whether the returned passages answer the question.
- No ANN index on `knowledge_chunks`: the KB is 19 chunks, and an exact scan is both faster and exact.
- `search.py` imports the `TextEmbedder` type from `ingest.py` rather than adding an embeddings module that is not in the spec tree; ingest owns the stored format that search reads.
- `scripts/seed.py` seeds only the knowledge base until HubSpot seeding is added in step 5; the Langfuse compose profile is added with tracing in step 6.
- Turn detection uses `livekit.agents.inference.TurnDetector` (audio model, lists Arabic) instead of the deprecated `livekit-plugins-turn-detector` multilingual text model, whose language list does not include Arabic; `v1` runs on LiveKit Inference and falls back to the local `v1-mini` at the free-plan limit.
- Barge-in uses `interruption_mode="vad"` by default: local, free and identical in dev and production; `adaptive` (LiveKit Inference, ignores backchannels like "uh-huh") is one config change away.
- Claude Haiku 5.5 runs with thinking disabled and effort `low`; the LiveKit Anthropic plugin has no option for these, so `ReasoningControlledLLM` attaches them to every request.
- The worker runs with `python -m livekit.agents start ... --dev`, because the built-in `cli.run_app` CLI is deprecated in livekit-agents 1.8.
- `search_knowledge_base` asks the LLM for the question in English: the KB is English, and e5 scores English queries higher than the same question in Arabic.
- Turn latency is read from LiveKit's per-message metrics (`e2e_latency`, `llm_node_ttft`, `tts_node_ttfb`, `end_of_turn_delay`, `transcription_delay`); on knowledge turns the first audio is the agent's short acknowledgement before the tool call.
- STT detects the language of every commit (no pinned language): pinning Arabic with English as a secondary language gave the same latency (transcription delay 0.89–1.00 s against 0.88–0.99 s) but tagged every English turn `ar`. Measured on Windows/WSL, not comparable with the Intel Mac bench.
- The caller's language is the script of the transcript when at least 80% of its letters are in one script, and the STT's detected language only for mixed-script turns: the plugin tags any final without a detected language `en`, which happened to every recovered Arabic turn in the tests.
- `EmptyCommitRecoveringStream` uses the pending partial transcript when Scribe realtime commits an empty transcript: measured on generated audio, an Arabic turn right after an English one came back empty in 7 of 12 English-to-Arabic switches across five runs although its partial held the whole sentence, so the caller's turn was lost; with the fix all 6 switches in two further runs were answered.
- The reply language note is added in `llm_node`, for every generation and only in that generation's context: `on_user_turn_completed` is skipped for typed and text-mode turns (an English question after an Arabic one was answered in Arabic) and does not cover the answer after a tool call; it also kept preemptive generation alive on switch turns (invalidations in the 7-turn audio run went from 5 to 0).
- One voice ("Sarah") for both languages, with the TTS language following the caller; `tts_voice_id_by_language` can override it per language. An Arabic reply synthesised this way was transcribed back word for word, and the bilingual greeting came back intact in both halves.
- The turn detector stays on for Arabic as well, with no VAD-only fallback: on generated audio with a 1.5 s mid-sentence pause, English was held as one turn (4 of 4 runs, but closed at the 3.0 s maximum delay) and Arabic was split at the pause (3 of 3, the same as VAD-only, which also split English); complete sentences ended 0.88–1.00 s after speech in both languages, so the detector costs Arabic nothing and VAD-only would only lose the English benefit. Measured on Windows/WSL, not comparable with the Intel Mac bench.
- About 0.55 s of the ~0.9 s transcription delay is the VAD silence window before the STT is flushed and about 0.35 s is Scribe's commit; this is the first thing to tune in the latency bench. Measured on Windows/WSL, not comparable with the Intel Mac bench.
- Test utterances with a pause are synthesised in one request with an SSML `<break>`: joining separately synthesised clips gave the first part sentence-final intonation, which an audio turn detector rightly treats as a finished turn.
- The greeting is one short Arabic line and one short English line ("أهلًا بك في لاركسبر. Hi, Arabic or English is fine."): 3.24 s with the production voice, down from 10.4 s, and both halves transcribe back intact. Measured on Windows/WSL, not comparable with the Intel Mac bench.
- HubSpot runs on `api-eu1.hubapi.com` with a service key holding only the contacts and tickets scopes; tested 2026-10-29: contacts, tickets, calls, and call-to-contact (194) and call-to-ticket (220) associations all work with those scopes, so calls are associated with the contact and every ticket, with no contact-only fallback.
- HubSpot requests time out after 4 s and are tried at most twice (429, 5xx and network errors only), so a failing CRM costs the caller seconds behind the spoken acknowledgement rather than a long silence; every tool turns a `HubSpotError` into an apology.
- Writes are two steps with the decision in code, not in the LLM: `create_ticket`, `request_human_callback` and a new `find_or_create_contact` only prepare a write and return the read-back; `save_confirmed_request` performs it only if the caller's very next turn is an explicit yes in English or Arabic without a negation or hedge ("yes, but…" and "لحظة" are not a yes).
- `conversation/confirmation.py` and `conversation/ticket_tools.py` are not in the spec tree: keeping the five spec tools plus the save step in `tools.py` would make it about 280 lines, against the 150-line rule; `confirmation.py` holds the write gate, `ticket_tools.py` the three ticket tools.
- Tool calls run one at a time (`parallel_tool_calls=False`): in a real run Claude saved the contact and proposed the ticket in parallel, the ticket saw no contact yet, and the caller heard that it had failed.
- The human callback is an URGENT ticket in the General inquiry category, confirmed like any other write, after which the agent says goodbye and ends the call with LiveKit's `EndCallTool` (`end_instructions=None`, so the goodbye is said once).
- The caller's phone comes from the room metadata key `caller_phone` when present (a phone gateway would set it; the Agents Playground does not), otherwise from what the caller says; Saudi numbers are normalised to E.164 before searching or saving.
- PII masking covers emails, phone numbers, Saudi national and iqama IDs (Latin and Arabic-Indic digits), and spelled-out read-backs: in the first real call log the agent's letter-by-letter email read-back passed the email pattern, so runs of six or more single characters and spoken symbols are masked too. It over-masks long ticket IDs, which the agent never speaks in full.
- The call summary is written by the same Claude model from the already-masked transcript, so no unmasked PII leaves the process for summarising; if the summary fails, the call is still logged with the transcript.
- Langfuse is the self-hosted v4 stack (web, worker, Postgres, ClickHouse, Redis, MinIO) under the compose profile `langfuse`, on port 3100 because 3000 is taken by another local Langfuse; it uses only `LANGFUSE_`-prefixed variables, since compose also reads this project's `.env`, whose `DATABASE_URL` is the pgvector URL, and its project, keys and user are created headlessly at first start.
- Tracing exports LiveKit's own OpenTelemetry spans (one `agent_session` trace per call, an `llm_request` span per model call with tokens and time to first token, a span per tool call) to Langfuse's OTLP endpoint through `LangfuseSpanExporter`, which masks PII in every string attribute before export (LiveKit's `allow_pii=False` would drop the conversation instead), adds each model call's cost from `model_prices_usd_per_million_tokens` so traces and evals use the same prices, and sets the trace's session (the room), pseudonymous user and languages.
- The trace user is `caller-` plus an HMAC-SHA256 of the caller's phone or contact id, keyed with the Langfuse secret key: stable per caller, but the number cannot be read from it or brute-forced without the key.
- LiveKit's `llm_node` and `llm_request_run` spans name the model but carry no tokens, and Langfuse counted them as generations with estimated tokens and cost (measured: 39 generations and about twice the real token count for 13 model calls); spans with a model but no usage are now exported as plain spans, leaving exactly one generation per model call.
- Phone masking skips ISO dates: in the first trace every `completion_start_time` became `[phone]T…`, which also removed time to first token from Langfuse.
- `save_confirmed_request` is now `record_caller_answer`, called after every answer to a read-back: with the old name Claude skipped it after a "no", so declined read-backs left no confirmation decision in the trace and a stale pending write.
- Langfuse v4 runs in events-only mode, so its legacy `/api/public/traces` endpoint returns 404; traces are read through `/api/public/v2/observations`.
- Evals run the real agent, LLM, knowledge base and HubSpot client in text mode against an in-memory HubSpot behind `httpx.MockTransport` (`evals/fake_hubspot.py`), with a simulated caller that answers whichever read-back is actually pending (`evals/simulated_call.py`); the deterministic checks (`evals/checks.py`) and the Claude Sonnet judge (`evals/judge.py`) are separate modules, which the spec tree does not list but the 150-line rule requires.
- Eval rules fixed before the first run: a scenario passes only if every deterministic check passes and all three judge scores are at least 4; a write passes only if it happened in a caller turn that is an explicit yes and called `record_caller_answer`; a reply's language is its majority script; Arabic replies may contain no digits, English replies no digits for prices, times or durations (identifiers such as invoice numbers are allowed).
- Claude Haiku 5.5 has no thinking budget (`budget_tokens` returns 400; it supports adaptive thinking only), so thinking off was compared with adaptive thinking at effort `low` on the same 10 caller turns (5 English, 5 Arabic, with tool turns): time to first token median 1.421 s against 2.001 s, mean 1.416 s against 3.017 s, max 1.896 s against 12.178 s, output tokens 2,314 against 3,351, no errors either way. That is over the rule set before measuring (keep thinking only if the median rises by at most 150 ms), so thinking stays off.
- With thinking off, `conversation/reply_filter.py` filters the LLM stream sentence by sentence: sentences that talk about the caller in the third person or about the reply language are the model thinking aloud and are dropped; a reply that opens in the wrong language is regenerated once with a firmer note, and later wrong-language sentences are dropped. Holding each sentence until it ends delays the first audio until the first sentence is complete.
- `pipeline/spoken_text.py` turns every sentence the voice speaks (LLM replies and the tools' read-backs, in `tts_node`) into words in the reply language: prices with the right riyal form, times, percentages, dates and years, P1–P3, and identifiers of four or more digits digit by digit; in Arabic, CSV, PDF, product names and spelled letters, "at" and "dot" take Arabic names. Cardinals come from `num2words`. The LLM is now told to write numbers as digits, because when it spelled them itself it got Arabic wrong ("ثلاثة وأربعين وأربعين" for 3344).
- Read-backs are spoken by the tools from fixed templates (`READ_BACKS`) in the caller's language and the tools return nothing, so LiveKit asks the LLM for no reply and it cannot add priorities or reasons; `create_ticket` and `request_human_callback` take a short phrase in the caller's language for the read-back, while HubSpot keeps the English subject.
- The caller's line is looked up in HubSpot as soon as the session starts (after `session.start`, so the greeting is not delayed), and every generation is told whether the caller is already identified or that their line is not on file, so the agent asks only for an email when it must.
- Caller language detection and the reply-language guard first remove product names, emails, spelled values and numbers (`prose_text`): an Arabic line containing an email address was below the 80% Arabic threshold, no language was detected and the read-back came out in English.
- `conversation/call_state.py`, `conversation/reply_filter.py`, `pipeline/spoken_text.py`, `evals/simulated_caller.py`, `evals/scenario_checks.py` and `evals/report.py` are not in the spec tree; each split keeps files near the 150-line rule (and `call_state.py` breaks the import cycle between the tools and the write gate).
- Eval changes for the second run, thresholds unchanged: the simulated caller answers clarifying questions from a per-scenario list and closes when asked "anything else?"; the transcript is read from the session history, because tools now speak the read-backs; `numbers_as_words` and the judge see the text handed to the voice; reply language is judged on prose only (no emails, ticket ids, numbers or product names); a new `one_question_per_turn` check counts question marks per agent turn; in the four scenarios where the caller's line is on file, "find_or_create_contact is called" became "the caller is identified as contact 101/303", because the line is now checked before any tool; the two handoff scenarios answer the callback reason before the identity line.
- Intel Macs cannot install torch 2.15 (no macOS x86_64 wheels since 2.2.2), and pinning 2.2.2 there does not work: it raises "Numpy is not available" with our numpy 2.5 and transformers 5.21 disables PyTorch below 2.6. On that platform only, pyproject leaves out sentence-transformers and torch, pins onnxruntime 1.23.2 (also its last Intel Mac release, needed by Silero) and `knowledge/onnx_models.py` runs the same models' published `onnx/model.onnx` exports with mean pooling and normalisation; every other platform resolves exactly the packages it did before (checked by resolving the exported lock for Linux x86_64 and macOS arm64 before and after). On Intel Macs the reranker scores are the cross-encoder's raw logits, which may differ in scale from `CrossEncoder.predict` but rank passages the same way. That module is not in the spec tree; it keeps `ingest.py` under 150 lines.
- `tests/test_onnx_parity.py` (marker `models`, skipped where sentence-transformers is not installed, so it runs on Linux and CI and downloads both models) checks the ONNX path against sentence-transformers: 10 English and Arabic sentences must reach cosine similarity above 0.999, and 5 question-passage pairs must rank in the same order. A local Linux run (Docker, linux/amd64, torch 2.15.0+cpu, onnxruntime 1.31.1, sentence-transformers 6.2.0) completed the embedding test with a minimum cosine of 1.000000; it was stopped before the reranker test, while it was still downloading the cross-encoder, so the reranker order is verified only by CI. In the same run, sentence-transformers and ONNX gave identical similarities to three decimals for the earlier suspicious Arabic question ("كيف أصدر فواتيري؟": English invoices 0.698, Arabic password 0.729, Arabic invoices 0.814), so preferring a same-language unrelated passage over a cross-language matching one is e5-small's own behaviour, not an ONNX difference; it finds the matching passage when one exists in the caller's language. The knowledge base was re-ingested on the Intel Mac with the ONNX embedder (19 chunks).
- Superseding the line on STT language detection: the STT is pinned to Arabic with English as the secondary language. Left to detect freely, Scribe transcribed the Arabic bench audio as Bulgarian ("Какъв е цената на месечната кутия?", tagged `bg`), so the agent had no reply language and no filter for that turn; the caller's language now comes from the transcript's script, so English turns tagged `ar` no longer matter.
- The privacy fix for ticket numbers was already in place (commit bdb25ea): `get_ticket_status` only reads the caller's own tickets and speaks the same fixed sentence, in the caller's language, for a ticket that belongs to someone else and for one that does not exist; one test covers both cases in both languages.
- The latency bench (`evals/latency_bench.py`) starts its own worker with the settings under test and a unique `AGENT_NAME`, opens one LiveKit Cloud room per language that dispatches only that worker, and streams the test WAVs in real time with silence in between, like a microphone. With `TURN_LATENCY_TOPIC` set, the worker publishes each turn's LiveKit metrics and each reply's filter hold on the room's data channel; without it nothing is published. `evals/bench_caller.py`, `evals/bench_report.py`, `evals/bench_setup.py` and `evals/reply_latency_bench.py` are not in the spec tree; they keep the bench files under 150 lines.
- The bench asks 20 distinct questions per language (33 new utterances, 1,328 ElevenLabs characters), four per language with 0.7-1.7 s mid-sentence pauses: with 3-4 utterances repeated, the model stopped calling `search_knowledge_base` and answered repeats from the conversation, which flattered the tool design.
- LiveKit's `llm_node_ttft` is timed on the output of our `llm_node`, after the reply filter, so the bench reports the model's time to first token as that minus the filter's hold, which `guarded_reply` now measures (first model text to first sentence let through, including a regenerated reply).
- The reply filter is unchanged: by voice it held the first sentence 0.14 s (English) and 0.20 s (Arabic) at p50, 0.19 s and 0.52 s at p90, and the ElevenLabs plugin in auto mode synthesises one sentence at a time with its own sentence tokenizer, so releasing part of a sentence earlier would only be buffered again in the plugin.
- Knowledge search runs before the model on each new caller turn (`kb_search_mode="every_turn"`), with the search tool kept behind the flag. In text mode the first text came 0.87 s (English) and 1.02 s (Arabic) after the caller's turn at p50, against 1.22 s and 1.11 s for the tool's acknowledgement and 2.17 s and 2.03 s for its answer; by voice the caller heard the answer at 3.56 s and 4.42 s at p50, against 5.50 s and 7.64 s with the tool. Conversation evals first fell to 18-21 of 32 against 21, all in ticket flows, because the search also ran after tool calls and on answers to read-backs and a miss carried the tool's "offer a ticket" instruction; it now runs only for a new caller turn with no pending write and a miss adds a neutral note, and both designs then passed 21 and 23 of 32 (final run on the shipped configuration: 24). `conversation/knowledge_first.py`, not in the spec tree, holds the two modes' prompt rules and the search before the model.
- The conversation evals show each search before the model as a `search_knowledge_base` call with its real result, from `CallState.knowledge_lookups`: without it the judge marked grounding down because "the transcript shows no tool call", although the passages were in the model's context.
- The silence wait is 0.3 s for both the VAD and the endpointing minimum (from 0.55 s and 0.5 s): on otherwise clean turns the end-of-turn delay fell from about 0.86-1.00 s to 0.57-0.82 s, and none of the eight utterances with 0.7-1.7 s pauses was cut off at either setting (the turn detector waited through them, up to its 3 s maximum). LiveKit's streaming turn detector needs at least 0.25 s of VAD silence.
- Correcting the earlier line on STT disconnects: ElevenLabs did not close the socket, our side did. LiveKit's `WebSocketMonitor` pings after 2 s without a server message and fails the connection after 6 s (the default `liveness_timeout`); Scribe sends nothing while the caller is silent, and its pongs took up to 3.2 s on this network (median 0.27 s, p99 1.75 s over 106 pings), so a slow pong closed a healthy session, the next audio write failed with "Cannot write to closing transport" and the cancelled receive task lost the close code. The STT now uses a 30 s liveness timeout (`stt_liveness_timeout_seconds`): in the bench reconnects fell from 8-13 per run to 0.
- The bench reports two end-to-end times: the agent's (LiveKit's, from the caller's last speech to the first agent audio leaving the worker) and the caller's (from the end of the bench's utterance to audible agent audio), which adds both network legs to LiveKit Cloud and audio buffering, 1.1-1.5 s at p50. LiveKit already subtracts the VAD silence from the end of speech.
- Each bench room is a real call to the agent, so it is logged to HubSpot like one: the bench runs on 2026-10-29/30 created 10 call engagements in the test portal.
- This step used 6,114 ElevenLabs characters (472 on the free tier before the upgrade, 5,642 after: 1,328 for test audio and 1,646-1,984 per 40-turn voice run), leaving 32,520, and $1.92 of Claude for nine conversation eval runs ($0.18-0.24 each); the text-mode timing runs were not metered.
- After a reconnect the STT resends the audio it sent since the last commit the server confirmed (`pipeline/stt_input.py`), starting at the first speech frame: with a forced mid-utterance drop the turn came back whole and the next turns were clean. Replaying the silence before it too (9.1 s in one burst) left Scribe with a backlog, since it works at about real time and commits what it has heard so far, so that commit and the next five were cut short or came back in English.
- The STT hears nothing before the caller's first words but 0.5 s of pre-roll (`stt_speech_rms_level` 150, `stt_speech_preroll_seconds`); a flush also opens the gate, so speech the VAD heard but this level misses is still sent. A Scribe session whose first 10 s held no speech, digital silence or quiet noise, committed the next three turns empty or cut short, with no language, in four of four probes, and a call always starts that way because the greeting plays first; this was why the second and third Arabic questions were lost in most bench runs. With the gate the same probes transcribed every turn.
- A commit with no detected language that repeats the previous commit's text is treated as empty (`drop_repeated_commit`): Scribe sent one for a commit of silence, and the agent would have answered the same question twice.
- Unanswered turns per 20 in the shipped configuration: English 1, Arabic 4 before the STT fixes; English 1, Arabic 3 with the liveness timeout and replay (no reconnects; the losses were the start-of-session problem); English 0, Arabic 0 with the speech gate as well. Heard by the caller at p50 / p90: English 3.56 / 6.87 s before, 3.70 / 5.97 s after, Arabic 4.42 / 7.71 s before, 3.53 / 5.40 s after; agent-measured: English 2.31 / 5.52 s and 2.30 / 4.48 s, Arabic 2.92 / 6.29 s and 2.50 / 4.08 s. The p90 after counts the turns that used to be lost, among them two pause utterances on which the turn detector waits up to 3 s.
- The ~1.2 s between the agent's first audio and what the caller hears is transport, not the agent or the bench: the bench now records the worker's wall-clock end of caller speech and start of speaking (same Mac clock), which split it into 0.53-0.62 s from the caller's last word to the worker's end of speech and 0.56-0.57 s from the worker's first frame to audible audio at the caller (p50). TTS audio starts audible (0.00-0.03 s below the speech level), and the round trip to LiveKit is 40-60 ms, but two plain LiveKit participants on this Mac, with no agent, measured 0.40 s one way at the median on a track with continuous audio and 0.63 s when the track starts after silence: that is WebRTC jitter buffering and pacing. The way in also carries the VAD's end-of-speech lag, which LiveKit's end-of-speech time does not subtract. An agent track that kept sending silence between replies would save about 0.2 s, but livekit-agents 1.8.10 has no supported option for it and patching RoomIO's private forwarding loop is not worth the fragility, so it is left as is.
- The bench no longer logs its rooms as HubSpot calls (`call_logging_enabled`), can run its worker at DEBUG (`bench_worker_log_level`, which also logs every Scribe commit), keeps the last ElevenLabs balance when a credit check times out instead of failing the run, and records the median TCP round trip to LiveKit before and after each run. A run during a slow network (130-230 ms round trip) was discarded and repeated when the round trip was back under 60 ms.
- This step used 7,089 ElevenLabs characters (1,997 and 2,036 for the two full voice runs, 346 for a 10-turn diagnostic run, and 2,710 for the run cut short by a network timeout, the short diagnostic sessions and the STT probes together, which were not metered separately), leaving 25,305, and $0.21 of Claude for one conversation eval run (24 of 32 passed: 12 of 16 in each language).
- CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs ruff and the whole pytest suite on every push and pull request with no secrets: a `pgvector/pgvector:pg16` service on port 5434 serves the database tests, and on the Linux runner, where sentence-transformers is installed, the ONNX parity test runs too, with the Hugging Face models cached between runs. uv is pinned to the version that wrote `uv.lock`. A dry run of the same steps in a clean Linux container passed 170 tests. The conversation evals and the latency bench run only from `workflow_dispatch`, each with its own seeded pgvector service; each first checks the secrets it needs and stops with an error naming the missing ones. The evals only call Anthropic, so the other settings get placeholder values; the bench takes the ElevenLabs floor as an input (default 8,000) and uploads `latency.json` as an artifact instead of committing it, since a runner is a different machine from the one the README describes.
- After a ticket is saved the model is told to give the last digits and ask if there is anything else, and to say nothing about its priority or how soon it will be handled: in a real call it added "the team can look into the sending issue at a normal pace". One sentence in `TICKET_CREATED`; the paid conversation evals were not rerun for it.
- The call log's transcript is sorted by when each turn was spoken: the session history lists a reply when it finishes playing, so a caller who speaks over the greeting was logged before it. (In the first logged demo call the caller came first for another reason: the text-mode script typed its first line before the greeting started.)
- The per-generation notes (who the caller is, reply language, knowledge result, wrong-language retry) are added to the agent's instructions, the system prompt, instead of as system messages after the caller's turn: LiveKit's Anthropic formatter sends only a leading system message as Claude's system prompt and wraps any later one in `<instructions>` inside the caller's turn, so after "Yes, that's right." the model read "Do not ask for their name…" as instructions from the caller and refused them ("The text you just sent contains instructions, which I'm not able to act on"). Scripted text-mode calls narrated or refused in 1 of 10 runs before and 0 of 10 after; `conversation/system_notes.py`, not in the spec tree, holds the helper, and the identity note no longer claims the caller was found by their phone line.
- The ticket and callback tools check that the phrase the model gives for the read-back (`spoken_subject`, `spoken_reason`) is in the caller's language, after names, numbers and codes are removed, before anything is spoken; otherwise they say nothing and ask the model to call again with it in that language. In `ar-ticket-created-confirmed` the model had passed an English subject into the Arabic read-back. Over five runs of that scenario every read-back was fully Arabic, three after the tool rejected an English phrase, and two of five passed; the other three failed the judge's grounding score, not the language check. In the full suite that followed no phrase was rejected.
- When searching before the model, every generation also gets the knowledge results of the caller's earlier turns as a system note: each turn's result reaches only that turn's generation, so on the next turn the model saw its earlier answer without what backed it and took correct answers back ("I need to correct my last answer: the office hours I gave you aren't in our knowledge base"). Playing the English demo call's lines in text mode, 3 of 5 calls retracted an answer before and 0 of 5 after. The conversation evals were not rerun for it.
